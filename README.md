# SpriteSquirter

A 3D village game built with [Godot 4](https://godotengine.org). Pick a start
location on an island floating in the sky, and your tiny villagers gather
lumber, stone and food, farm wheat, raise surreal buildings and have
children. Every night at midnight the **goblins raid**: your villagers,
warriors and wizards fight them off, helped by a giant, gloomy tortoise that
stomps, throws and eats goblins. You cast **fireballs**, **rain**, **roots**
and a **blood sacrifice** with magicko. Make your own islands in the **map editor**. The long-term plan is
to grow it into an RTS / tower-defense game (see [docs/ROADMAP.md](docs/ROADMAP.md)).

The art direction is **surreal and cel shaded**: flat bands of light, black
ink outlines, bold colours and strange buildings. See
[docs/ART_STYLE.md](docs/ART_STYLE.md).

Everything is generated by code: buildings, trees, textures, even the
tortoise model. No 3D modelling tools are needed to run or change it.

## Getting the latest version

The newest build is always at this one link (it is the `main` branch):

<https://github.com/PrettyGoodSoftware1000/SpriteSquirter/archive/refs/heads/main.zip>

**Version number.** The version is shown in the bottom-right corner of the
main menu ("Version 0.10"). It goes up by 0.01 with every update to the
game, so you can tell at a glance whether you are running the newest
download. It is set as `config/version` in `project.godot`.

## Running it

1. Download **Godot 4.7** (standard build, not .NET) from
   <https://godotengine.org/download>. It is free and needs no install, so just unzip and run it.
2. In the Godot Project Manager click **Import**, choose this folder's
   `project.godot`, then **Import & Edit**.
3. Press **F5** (or the ▶ button) to play.

The first import takes a few seconds while Godot builds its cache in `.godot/`.

## Playing

The main menu offers a **random island** (Small 192 m, Medium 384 m or
Large 768 m square; the islands are about 170, 350 and 700 m across), your
**saved maps**, and the **map editor**. Menus and text scale with the window
size (2.4 times bigger at 3840 x 2160 than at 1600 x 900).

1. **Choose a start location.** Numbered banners mark them; click one. On a
   random island each banner is an existing village, which becomes yours. On
   a map from the editor you start with 8 villagers and no buildings.
2. **Your villagers work by themselves.** Grown-ups fell trees (lumber),
   break up boulders (stone) and forage near the village (food), and every
   grown-up adds a trickle of magicko. Workers carry each load to the
   nearest **Storehouse** (or to the middle of the village while you have
   none); it only counts once it arrives. Boulders come in small boulder
   fields dotted over the island. Trees and boulders run out, so the
   workers move on. A worker carries a small load (2 units) home on its
   back. Once you have a **Blacksmith**, workers use wheelbarrows, which
   take three times as much.
3. **Build.** Press **B** or **Backspace** for the build menu, pick a
   building, and place its outline (**R** rotates it). The cost is paid up
   front and the villagers come and build it: up to 5 work on each
   building (your **Builders** first, then unassigned Workers), and a
   second building gets 5 of its own. The
   more a building costs, the longer it takes: 10 seconds of one
   villager's work for each unit of resource, so a Home (55) takes 10
   villagers just under a minute. The building rises out of the ground as
   they work.
   The buildings have jobs: a **Home** (or Mushroom cottage) adds housing, a
   **Storehouse** is where resources are carried, a **Town guard** trains
   warriors (and archers), the **Singing rocks** train wizards, a
   **Necromancer tower** turns wizards into necromancers, and a
   **Blacksmith** makes wheelbarrows and does research.
   **Asset names and gameplay names.** Every building has an asset name
   (its model: Stacked houses, Moon tower, Skull tower, Treehouse...) and
   a gameplay name (what it does: Storehouse, Town guard, Necromancer
   tower, Home...). `Names.txt`, next to `project.godot`, pairs them up,
   one `Asset name = Gameplay name` per line; change the name after the =
   and that model takes on that job. The asset names never change.
   **Treehouses and rope bridges.** There are four treehouses (Treehouse,
   Lookout treehouse, Twin treehouse, Great treehouse): houses on a deck
   up a bright-leaved tree, with a ladder. Pick **Rope bridge** in the
   build menu and every treehouse a bridge can join lights up green;
   click one, then another up to 35 m away (the ones in reach stay lit),
   and the villagers hang a bridge between the two decks. It costs 1
   lumber a metre. Villagers climb up and walk over a bridge when it is
   on their way, and idle ones stroll across.
   **Bones and necromancers.** Whenever a villager, goblin or ogre dies
   the village gets 2 to 4 **bones** (the bone counter in the top bar).
   Build a Necromancer tower (it costs bones too) and ask for
   necromancers in the Jobs row: a wizard trains at the tower for 5
   minutes and comes out in black robes. A necromancer still throws
   firebolts, and raises one dead villager or enemy lying nearby as a
   **skeleton** warrior or archer, for 2 bones. A skeleton fights for you
   with half the health and damage of whoever it was, and crumbles after
   3 minutes; then the necromancer can raise another.
   **Research.** With a Blacksmith, press **U** (or the Research button)
   and pick something to research. It is paid for up front and one thing
   is researched at a time: **Sharper swords** (5 minutes; warriors do 50%
   more damage), **Bows** (3 minutes; unlocks archers), and **Pitchforks**,
   **Mining picks** and **Axes** (2 minutes each; food, stone or lumber is
   gathered twice as fast).
   Your first **Storehouse** is free: it appears on the start location
   when you found the village.
   **Workers and set jobs.** **Workers** in the Jobs row is the number of
   villagers you have not set to anything: they gather whatever is most
   needed by themselves. In the **Gather** row, **+** takes one Worker
   and makes it a Lumberjack, Miner, Farmer or **Builder** at once (never
   a warrior, archer or wizard), and **-** sends one back to the Workers.
   Builders only build: they are first onto every building site and wait
   when there is nothing to build.
4. **Jobs.** Grown-ups are workers unless you ask for warriors or wizards
   in the **Jobs** row under the resource counter (the - and + buttons).
   Each Town guard allows 4 warriors and trains one at a time, taking a
   minute each; each Singing rocks allows 2 wizards and trains one at a
   time, taking 3 minutes each. More buildings train side by side. A grey
   bar fills over the count while recruits train. Lower the number and
   warriors or wizards go straight back to work; they have to train again
   to return. **Wizards** make 5 magicko a minute each and throw tiny
   **firebolts** at goblins from a distance, faster than a warrior's sword,
   sometimes setting them alight. Once **Bows** are researched an Archers
   count appears: each Town guard allows 2 **archers** (90 seconds each),
   who shoot goblins from 22 m.
5. **Feed everyone.** Each villager (children too) eats 1 food a minute,
   each warrior 3 and each archer or wizard 2; the counter shows how much
   is eaten. Your creature eats too: 20 food a minute when full grown, and
   in proportion to its size before that (3 a minute at the start).
   With no food, no children are born, and after a minute without food
   someone starves every 30 seconds until there is food again.
6. **Farm.** Pick **Wheat field** in the build menu, then hold the left
   mouse button and drag out a square on the ground (1 lumber per 10 m²).
   Villagers sow it, the wheat grows and turns gold, and they reap it for
   much more food than foraging gives. Then they sow it again.
7. **Grow.** A tiny villager is born every *birth timer* seconds while there
   is housing (10, plus what your Homes add) and food. Children grow up in a minute
   and then start working.
8. **Survive the night.** The night is half as long as the daylight. Halfway
   through it every goblin leaves its cave and marches on your village,
   swimming rivers on the way. They attack villagers first, then buildings,
   and go home in the morning. Every cave that was there when the game
   began keeps spawning goblins (1 a minute at first, half a goblin more
   per minute every night; both in the dev menu).
   Once a raid has 15 goblins an **ogre** with a spiked club joins it, and another for
   every 15 more: ten times a goblin's health, three times its damage, too
   big to pick up or throw, and only singed by fire.
   During a raid a **giant bat** flies out of a cave, snatches one of
   your villagers at random and carries it back to the cave for good. A
   fireball or chain lightning brings it down, and a villager it was
   carrying drops and lives.
   A goblin has the same health, damage and attack speed as a grown-up
   villager. Every grown-up in the village turns out to fight raiders
   that come within 50 m of its centre (and any goblin that wanders
   within 25 m, raid or no raid); children run, and **warriors** (leather
   armour, helmet, sword and shield; four times the health and twice the
   damage) go looking for raiders. The **tortoise** helps by itself: goblins
   within its reach get a **stomp** that hurts everything round its foot
   (it never stomps when a villager or one of your buildings would be
   caught), or it scoops up three to five of them and **throws** or
   **eats** them. During a raid it walks to the raiders unless you have
   sent it somewhere. With goblins about it moves 20% faster, scowls and
   opens its mouth. Nobody runs away from the tortoise any more.
   **Your creature.** Choose a **tortoise** or a **squirrel** in the main menu.
   The squirrel is a nervous wreck (it shivers, wrings its paws and keeps
   glancing over its shoulder) and 30% faster; the tortoise has twice the
   health. Either starts at 15% of its full size and grows for 30 minutes,
   getting faster, stronger and tougher as it does (a small one cannot pick
   goblins up or flatten buildings, and stays out of raids unless you send
   it). It stops growing while the village has no food. Goblins attack a
   creature that comes close; at no health it is knocked out for 45
   seconds and then gets up with half its health. Left alone it heals.
   **What it does by itself.** It wanders a little way from where you last
   sent it and comes back. When goblins raid, or hang about within 50 m of
   the village, it chases them, turning 30% faster in a fight. A line of
   text floats up from its head when it does something ("The creature ate
   2 goblins").
   **What it learns.** Each time you cast Fireball, Rain, Roots, Chain
   Lightning or Blood Sacrifice the creature gains 25 knowledge of that spell; at 100 it has
   learned it and casts it by itself when it suits (fireball at a knot of
   enemies away from your buildings, roots on a crowd of raiders, rain on a
   fire in the village, a blood sacrifice when magicko has run dry and
   there are a dozen or more villagers). Each of its spells then rests for
   3 minutes. Its fireball does your own villagers and buildings only a
   tenth of the harm. For every 100 lumber, stone or food your villagers
   carry into the store it gains 25 knowledge of harvesting that; once
   learned it pulls up a tree or a boulder (or reaps ripe wheat) and
   carries it to the Storehouse, 25 at a time when full grown.
   **Bless and Curse.** Press the **Bless** key (golden sparkles, 10
   magicko) and the top of the screen says what the creature last did;
   press the key again, or click the creature, within 30 seconds of that
   action and it becomes keener on doing it again. **Curse** (red and
   creepy, 10 magicko) works the same way and makes it less keen. Every action starts at 50 out of 100 and moves 10 a time; at
   0 it never does it. The creature cannot learn these two.
9. **Cast spells** from the spell bar at the bottom (keys 1 to 7, then click): **Fireball** blows
   things up and starts fires, **Rain** puts fires out and soaks the ground
   so it can't burn for a while (and makes wheat under the cloud grow three
   times as fast while it is wet), and **Roots** grows a glowing green patch
   of roots for 20 seconds: goblins and the tortoise in it are held where
   they stand, and your own people wade through at a quarter of their
   speed. The cost in magicko (the blue droplet) is under each spell.
   **Chain Lightning** strikes whoever is nearest the click and jumps
   on through up to six things, each within 9 m of the last: goblins,
   ogres, bats and your own people alike. Anything wet (rained on in the
   last 20 seconds, or standing in a river or lake) takes double.
   **Blood Sacrifice** costs none: click near one of your villagers and
   they die, and magicko comes in twice as fast for 30 seconds (the counter
   turns red and drips while it lasts). A fireball crater is bare stone; it
   only fills with water when it breaks into water nearby.

The counter at the top shows lumber (log), stone, food (wheat), people
(and housing), magicko (blue droplet), the day, the time to the next birth
and how much food is eaten each minute. Days turn from dawn to
daytime, evening and night.

## Map editor

Start it from the main menu with **New map in the editor** (it uses the map
size chosen above it) or **Edit map** on a saved map. A new map begins as a
random island with trees and rocks, but no villages or buildings: in the
game, villagers build everything.

| Tool (key) | Action |
|---|---|
| Raise, Lower, Flatten, Smooth (1–4) | Sculpt the terrain; `[` and `]` change the brush size |
| Expand island (5) | Hold and draw out over the sky to grow the island, in any shape |
| Sand (6) | Paint sand |
| Grass (7) | Paint grass; clears sand, stone and dirt, and fills in water |
| Water (8) | Flood the ground at the height where you clicked; the ground is flattened under it with a bank round it, so ponds can sit up on hills |
| Start location (9) | Click to place a numbered start banner (1 to 8 per map) |
| Goblin cave (0) | Click to place a cave; R / Shift+R turn it first. Goblins move in when the game starts |
| Trees | Hold to plant trees inside the brush |
| Rocks | Click or hold to place rocks |
| Erase | Hold to remove trees, rocks, start locations and caves inside the brush |
| Cut cliff | Click on low ground and drag into a hill: it is sliced down to that height, leaving a sheer cliff nobody can climb |
| Goblin path | Draw from a goblin cave to a start location (or back): raiders from that cave loosely follow it. Several paths can join the same two; each has its own colour, shown only while this brush is picked. Shift+click removes one |

Water flows: lower the ground next to a river or lake and the water runs
in, and wherever water reaches the edge of the island it pours over as a
waterfall. When the land brush reaches the edge of the map square, the
editor asks whether to make the map bigger (64 m more on every side, up to
1024 m); nothing on the map moves.

Name the map and press **Save** (or Ctrl+S); a map needs at least one start
location. **Save and play** starts a game on it straight away. Maps are
saved as compressed files in Godot's user folder under `maps/` (on Windows
`%APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\SpriteSquirter\maps`); **Open maps folder** in
the main menu opens it, so maps can be copied and shared.

**Slopes.** Villagers, goblins and the creature slow down a lot walking
uphill, and nobody can climb a sheer cliff. Villagers and goblins plan
their way to keep off steep ground and round cliffs and water, unless
there is no other way.

## Balancing

`balancing.txt`, in the game's folder next to `project.godot`, holds the
numbers you may want to tune: the health and damage of villagers,
warriors, archers, wizards, goblins, ogres and the creatures, building
health, what every building costs and how long building takes, bones,
necromancers and skeletons, how often the goblins build, chain
lightning, the giant bat, how steep a slope slows or stops walkers,
how many villagers build one building, fireball damage, how much knowledge the creature needs to learn
each thing, the cooldown of each spell it casts, and the size of a bless
or curse. Open it in any text editor, change a number, save, and start
the game again. Each line reads `name = number`, with a note after `#`.

## What's in the world

- **Floating island:** a noise-based island with hills, lakes and a rocky
  underside, hanging in the sky with smaller islands drifting around it.
  Two rivers rise at a spring and pour over the edge as waterfalls.
- **Surreal buildings:** treehouse Homes joined by rope bridges, the
  crystal tower, the moon tower, the mushroom house, the singing rocks,
  the store-in-a-tree, the winged windmill and the skull-topped
  Necromancer tower. Your villagers build more from the build menu.
- **Goblin buildings:** every 5 minutes the goblins start a building of
  their own near a cave: a tent, war hut, forge, potion hut or
  watchtower of planks, rags and bones. A few goblins build it, going
  back to the cave for what they need. They have no use yet; a fireball
  or the creature's feet knock them down.
- **Villagers and goblins:** villagers wander their villages; goblins
  loiter around a cave and their huts, far from the villages, until the
  midnight raid. Both catch fire and get flung by explosions; the
  tortoise's footsteps squash goblins and strangers but not your own people.
- **The creatures:** a 12 m gloomy tortoise of about 110,000 triangles and
  an 11 m nervous squirrel of about 65,000, on the same
  skeleton. Walking is procedural: feet plant on the terrain with two-bone
  IK, and the body, arms, head, jaw and tail react.
- **Fireball:** thrown from your cursor. It explodes, digs a crater, breaks
  buildings and trees into physics debris (Jolt), and sets fires that
  spread, char buildings and eventually collapse them.
- **Rain:** a storm cloud that puts out fires, burning buildings and burning
  villagers, and leaves the ground too wet to burn for a while.
- **Roots:** glowing green roots in a 9 m circle for 20 seconds: goblins
  and the tortoise are held, your own people slowed.
- **Cel shading:** sunlight that is either on or off, with halftone dots
  where light meets shadow, dark blue-violet shadows, ink outlines, pen
  strokes hatched over the ground, a sun that never sits overhead, weather
  moods and swappable nature palettes (the default is "Alpine").
- **Detail up close:** villagers, warriors and goblins switch to detailed
  models with inked faces, hands, belts and trims when the camera is
  within about 60 m, and back to simple ones when they are small.

### Regenerating assets

The textures and the creature are generated files that are checked in, so
you only need these when you want to change them:

```bash
godot --path . -s tools/bake_textures.gd
```

```bash
pip install numpy scikit-image
```

```bash
python3 tools/bake_creature.py tortoise
```

```bash
python3 tools/bake_creature.py squirrel
```

### Smoke test

A headless run through the menu, a random game (gathering, births,
building, rain), the editor (save, list, load) and a saved-map game:

```bash
godot --headless --path . res://tools/smoke_test.tscn
```

It prints `SMOKE TEST PASSED` or the checks that failed, and exits with 0 or 1.

### Conventions

- GDScript with static types everywhere (`var x := ...`, typed arguments).
- One `class_name` per script. Shared systems are reached through the `Game`
  autoload instead of node paths.
- Units are metres. Local +Z is "forward" for the creature and buildings.
- Tunable numbers are `const` or `@export` at the top of each script.

## Tech notes

- **Engine:** Godot 4.7, Forward+ renderer, Jolt physics.
- **Performance:** built for a mid-to-high-end desktop GPU. Grass is the
  heaviest part; lower it in the dev menu (~, then G). Debris pieces fade out after about
  10 s, and the number of fires and fire lights is capped.
- Trees, buildings and villagers are still individual nodes (about 300
  trees, 40 buildings, 100 villagers). Instancing them is on the roadmap
  for RTS-scale unit counts.
