# SpriteSquirter

A 3D village game built with [Godot 4](https://godotengine.org). Pick a start
location on an island floating in the sky, and your tiny villagers gather
lumber, stone and food, farm wheat, raise surreal buildings and have
children. Every night at nightfall the **gooblins raid**: your villagers,
warriors and mages fight them off, helped by a giant, gloomy tortoise that
stomps, throws and eats gooblins. You cast **fireballs**, **rain**, **roots**
and a **blood sacrifice** with magicko. Make your own islands in the **map editor**. The long-term plan is
to grow it into an RTS / tower-defense game (see [docs/ROADMAP.md](docs/ROADMAP.md)).

The art direction is **surreal and cel shaded**: flat bands of light, black
ink outlines, bold colours and strange buildings. See
[docs/ART_STYLE.md](docs/ART_STYLE.md).

Everything is generated by code: buildings, trees, textures, even the
tortoise model. No 3D modelling tools are needed to run or change it.

## Getting the latest version

The newest build is always at this one link (it is the `main` branch):

<https://github.com/PrettyGoodSoftware1000/SpriteSquirter/archive/refs/heads/main.zip>

**Version number.** The version is shown in the bottom-right corner of the
main menu ("Version 0.10"). It goes up by 0.01 with every update to the
game, so you can tell at a glance whether you are running the newest
download. It is set as `config/version` in `project.godot`.

## Running it

1. Download **Godot 4.7** (standard build, not .NET) from
   <https://godotengine.org/download>. It is free and needs no install, so just unzip and run it.
2. In the Godot Project Manager click **Import**, choose this folder's
   `project.godot`, then **Import & Edit**.
3. Press **F5** (or the ▶ button) to play.

The first import takes a few seconds while Godot builds its cache in `.godot/`.

## Playing

The main menu offers a **random island** (Small 192 m, Medium 384 m or
Large 768 m square; the islands are about 170, 350 and 700 m across), your
**saved maps**, and the **map editor**. The version is shown under the
title. Menus and text scale with the window size (2.4 times bigger at
3840 x 2160 than at 1600 x 900). The main menu also sets how many
**neutral villages** there will be (see below), and its **Dev menu**
button (or ~) opens the gameplay sliders, so they can be set before a game
starts; they carry over into it.

1. **Choose a start location.** Numbered banners mark them; click one. A
   random island is just its start locations and one gooblin cave: nothing
   is built anywhere, and your first **Storehouse** goes up free where you
   click. A map from the editor is the same. The village starts with 20
   villagers, and 100 each of lumber, stone, food and magicko (`start_...`
   in `balancing.txt`). While you choose, a box under the message sets how
   many neutral villages take other start locations.
2. **Your villagers work by themselves.** Grown-ups fell trees (lumber),
   break up boulders (stone) and farm wheat fields (food: there is no other
   way to get it, so draw a field early), and every
   grown-up adds a trickle of magicko (1 a minute: everyone born in your
   village is a **worshiper** of your creature). Workers carry each load to the
   nearest **Storehouse** (food may go to a **Granary** too; with no
   Storehouse, to the middle of the village); it only counts once it
   arrives. Each Storehouse holds 300 each of lumber, stone, food and
   bones, and each Granary 300 more food: anything over that is lost.
   Magicko and **animus** have no limit; the village gets 1 animus a
   minute to begin with (`animus_per_minute`) and each **Shrine** it
   builds makes 1 more (Singing Stones that come with the village site
   make none). Boulders come in small boulder
   fields dotted over the island. Trees and boulders run out, so the
   workers move on; a boulder holds a lot of stone and crumbles away
   slowly once it is used up. A worker carries a small load (2 units) home on its
   back. Once **Wheelbarrows** are researched at a Blacksmith, workers
   carry three times as much (fey villagers carry theirs by magic: no
   wheelbarrow to see).
3. **Build.** Press **B** or **Backspace** for the build menu, pick a
   building, and place its outline (**R** rotates it) anywhere inside your
   village's **influence**: a wall of light shows its edge while you place
   a building, or hold the mouse over a Storehouse. It reaches 22.5 m from
   the start location and 3 m further for every villager (82.5 m with the
   20 villagers a village starts with; both in the dev menu). The cost is paid up
   front and the villagers come and build it: up to 5 work on each
   building (your **Builders** first, then unassigned Workers), and a
   second building gets 5 of its own. The
   more a building costs, the longer it takes: 5 seconds of one
   villager's work for each unit of resource, so a Home (55) takes 5
   villagers just under a minute. The building rises out of the ground as
   they work.
   The buildings have jobs: a **Home** (or Mushroom cottage) adds housing, a
   **Storehouse** is where resources are carried and houses 25 people, a
   **Granary** (the Windmill) stores food, a **Town guard** (the Tall
   house) trains warriors (and archers), a **Wizardry tower** (the Moon
   tower) trains mages, a **Shrine** (the Singing rocks) makes animus
   and is where a dead creature is brought back to life, a **Necromancer
   tower** turns mages into necromancers, a **Storm Spire** (the Crystal
   tower) trains Storm Banshees and strikes enemies with chain lightning,
   and a **Blacksmith** makes wheelbarrows and does research. The dev menu's construction rate makes
   all your building go faster or slower.
   **Asset names, gameplay names and in-game names.** Every building has
   an asset name (its model: Stacked houses, Moon tower, Skull tower,
   Treehouse...), a gameplay name (what it does: Storehouse, Town guard,
   Necromancer tower, Home...) and an in-game name (what players see it
   called, such as Wizardry tower). `Names.txt`, next to `project.godot`,
   pairs them up, one `Asset name = Gameplay name = In-game name` per
   line; change the gameplay name and that model takes on that job. A
   building with no in-game name is shown as "Name missing". The asset
   names never change, and players never see them: the build menu shows
   one, as a tooltip, only while the dev menu is open. The build menu
   shows a picture of each building. **StoreHouseAlt** (the cleaned-up
   storehouse model, in the menu as "Storehouse Alt") is a **Showpiece**:
   it costs nothing to build and does nothing (`cost_showpiece_...`).
   **Treehouses and rope bridges.** There are four treehouses (Treehouse,
   Lookout treehouse, Twin treehouse, Great treehouse): houses on a deck
   up a tree, with a ladder; their leaves take the colours of the nature
   palette, like the trees around them. While you place a treehouse,
   see-through bridges show which treehouses it could be joined to from
   there. Pick **Rope bridge** in the
   build menu and every treehouse a bridge can join lights up green;
   click one, then another up to 35 m away (the ones in reach stay lit),
   and the villagers hang a bridge between the two decks. It costs 1
   lumber a metre. Villagers climb up and walk over a bridge when it is
   on their way, and idle ones stroll across.
   **Bones and necromancers.** Whenever a villager, gooblin or ogre dies
   the village gets 2 to 4 **bones** (the bone counter in the top bar).
   Build a Necromancer tower (it costs bones too) and ask for
   necromancers in the village panel: a mage trains at the tower for 75
   seconds and comes out in black robes. A necromancer throws purple
   firebolts (half a mage's harm), and raises one dead villager or
   enemy lying within 32 m as a **skeleton** warrior or archer, for 2
   bones: 2 seconds beside the body, then 3 seconds' rest. It keeps one
   skeleton up at a time; a skeleton fights for you with half the health
   and damage of whoever it was, and crumbles after 3 minutes (or dies),
   and then the necromancer can raise another. All of this is in
   `balancing.txt` (`necromancer_...`, `skeleton_...`, `raise_bones`).
   **Research.** Click a Blacksmith (or press **U**) and pick something
   to research. It is paid for up front, and each Blacksmith researches
   one thing at a time (two Blacksmiths, two things at once; the same for
   Shrines and spells, and for Wizardry towers): **Sharper swords** (5 minutes; warriors do 50%
   more damage), **Wheelbarrows**,
   and **Pitchforks**, **Mining picks** and **Axes** in three tiers each
   (food, stone or lumber gathered 50% faster after the third;
   `tool_tier_bonus` a tier; a tier shows in the menu once the one
   before it is researched, says how much it adds to the base gathering
   speed, and researched upgrades leave the menu). Click a **Wizardry tower** for **Overcast**
   (10 animus): any spell can be cast once while it cools down, for twice
   the magicko.
   Your first **Storehouse** is free: it appears on the start location
   when you found the village.
   **The village panel** (top left). At the top, your people and the room
   you have for them (a bar fills behind the count towards the next
   birth) and the time of day. **Workers** is the number of villagers you
   have not set to anything: they gather whatever is most needed by
   themselves. Below it, **Builders**, then each resource with its income
   over the last minute: only what came in. Paying for a building (taken
   from the stores the moment it is placed), research or a spell does not
   change it. Food's can go below zero, since everyone eats all the time. Beside lumber, stone and food,
   **+** takes one Worker and makes it a Lumberjack, Miner or Farmer at
   once (Builders the same way; never a warrior, archer or mage), and
   **-** sends one back to the Workers. Hold the mouse over a + or - to see
   what it does.
   Builders only build: they are first onto every building site and wait
   when there is nothing to build. Only Builders and unassigned Workers
   build; set one to another job and it drops the building at once. Farmers sow and reap fields twice as fast as other workers. No job's **+** works unless there is a free Worker
   for it (one not already promised to a warrior, archer or mage you
   asked for); a necromancer needs a free mage.
4. **Jobs.** Grown-ups are workers unless you ask for warriors or mages
   at the bottom of the village panel (the - and + buttons).
   The count beside each, such as **Warriors 1/4**, is how many are
   trained or training (it goes up the moment you press +) of the most
   your buildings allow: 0/0 with no Town guard, 0/4 as soon as one
   stands.
   Each Town guard allows 4 warriors and trains one at a time, taking
   15 seconds each; each Wizardry tower allows 4 mages and trains one
   at a time, taking 45 seconds each (the dev menu's training time slider
   stretches or shortens all of them). Recruits are taken from the
   unassigned Workers. More buildings train side by side. A grey
   bar fills over the count while recruits train. Lower the number and
   warriors or mages go straight back to work; they have to train again
   to return. (Mages were called wizards before 0.61; the code and
   `balancing.txt` still say wizard.) Hold the mouse over the magicko in
   the village panel to see where it comes from (so much from the mages,
   so much from the villagers, the fey, the worshipers in neutral villages
   and a Blood Sacrifice). **Mages** make 5 magicko every 2 minutes each (`wizard_magicko`, 2.5 a minute; a worshiper makes 1 a minute, the dev menu's Magicko rate) and throw tiny
   **firebolts** at gooblins from a distance, faster than a warrior's sword,
   sometimes setting them alight. A **Lumber mill** (the Stacked houses)
   trains **archers**: each allows 4 (`archers_per_mill`; there are no
   bows to research any more), who shoot gooblins from 22 m, and stores
   150 more lumber (`lumber_mill_holds`). Click a Lumber mill to research
   **The Fancy Arrows**: archers' and Fey Archers' arrows do 25% more
   damage (`fancy_arrows_damage`). Every arrow (gooblin archers' too)
   leaves a short bright streak behind it, so it shows in flight, at
   night too. Archers shoot at buildings and caves rather than walk up to
   hack at them.
   **Necromancers** are mages trained on: pressing + for a necromancer
   gives up that mage's place at once (one fewer mage asked for), so the
   Wizardry tower can start on the next mage while the necromancer
   trains. (Archers, Fey Archers and Storm Banshees are trained from
   workers, so nobody's place is given up for them.)
   Each **Storm Spire** (the Crystal tower) allows 2 **Storm Banshees**
   (40 seconds each): winged villagers (dressed in purple for now) who
   blow a gust of wind every 6 seconds down a row 10 m wide in front of
   them, which pushes every attacking gooblin in it 20 m back and knocks it
   over (it is not hurt). The Spire itself makes 10 **Storm Essence** a
   minute (the violet bolt in the village panel), and strikes any enemy
   that comes within 50 m with **chain lightning**, no more often than
   every 20 seconds, for 25 Storm Essence a strike (all in
   `balancing.txt`).
5. **Feed everyone.** Each villager (children too) eats 1 food a minute,
   each warrior 3 and each archer, mage or Storm Banshee 2; the counter shows how much
   is eaten. Your creature eats too: 20 food a minute when full grown, and
   in proportion to its size before that, but only half that until it is
   half grown (1.5 a minute at the start).
   With no food, no children are born, and after a minute without food
   someone starves every 30 seconds until there is food again. "Out of
   food" floats up over the Storehouse (no oftener than every 45 seconds,
   `food_warning_seconds`).
6. **Farm.** Pick **Wheat field** in the build menu, then hold the left
   mouse button and drag out a square on the ground (1 lumber per 10 m²).
   Villagers sow it, the wheat grows and turns gold (in 200 seconds,
   `crop_grow_seconds`, divided by the dev menu's crop growth rate), and
   they reap it for food. Hold the mouse over a field to see how far along
   it is (the share of the way to harvest). Then they sow it again.
   Fields are the only source of food.
7. **Grow.** A tiny villager is born every *birth timer* seconds while there
   is housing (10, plus what your Homes add) and food. Children grow up in a minute
   and then start working.
8. **Survive the night.** The night is half as long as the daylight. Halfway
   through it every gooblin leaves its cave and marches on your village,
   swimming rivers on the way. They attack villagers first, then buildings,
   and go home in the morning. Every cave that was there when the game
   began keeps spawning gooblins (1 a minute, the dev menu's gooblin
   spawn, and 2 more for every neutral village there was at the start),
   and every gooblin building finished near a cave adds
   `goblin_rate_per_building` a minute more there. Only their buildings
   make them spawn faster; the nights alone do not.
   **Ogres and giant bats come from gooblin buildings.** Every **war hut**
   the gooblins finish adds an **ogre** with a spiked club (ten times a
   gooblin's health, three times its damage, too big to pick up or throw,
   and only singed by fire); if it dies, the hut sends another at the next
   nightfall. From the third night on, every **watchtower** sends a
   **giant bat** with each raid, which snatches one of
   your villagers at random and carries it back to a cave for good. Knock
   the building down and no more come. A
   fireball or chain lightning brings it down, and so does a single arrow
   or mage's firebolt: archers and mages always go for a bat they can
   see first. A villager it was carrying drops and lives.
   The gooblins have a giant of their own, the **balrog**: a horned demon
   that hatches at one of their caves and grows just as your creature
   does. Until it is full grown it only guards its cave, attacking whoever
   comes within 40 m of it. Full grown, it marches with every nightfall
   raid and stamps your buildings down. From half its full size it spits
   a **fireball** every 20 seconds (half as strong as yours). It has
   wings but cannot fly, and it walks a little slower than a gooblin.
   Your creature and the balrog fight each other with their fists; your
   archers, mages and everyone else join in, and fireballs and chain
   lightning hurt it. When it dies the village gets bones, and another
   hatches ten minutes later.
   A gooblin has the same health, damage and attack speed as a grown-up
   villager. Every grown-up in the village turns out to fight raiders
   that come within 50 m of its centre (and any gooblin that wanders
   within 25 m, raid or no raid); children run, and **warriors** (leather
   armour, helmet, sword and shield; four times the health and twice the
   damage) go looking for raiders. The **tortoise** helps by itself: gooblins
   within its reach get a **stomp** that hurts everything round its foot
   (it never stomps when a villager or one of your buildings would be
   caught), or it scoops up three to five of them and **throws** or
   **eats** them. During a raid it walks to the raiders unless you have
   sent it somewhere. With gooblins about it moves 20% faster, scowls and
   opens its mouth. Nobody runs away from the tortoise any more.
   **Your creature.** Choose a **tortoise**, a **squirrel** or a **raven**
   in the main menu. (The raven is the owner's two Meshy ravens since 0.61:
   one walking, one flying, swapped as it takes off and lands, each moved
   by its own animation clips; see `RavenGiant`.)
   The squirrel is a nervous wreck (it shivers, wrings its paws and keeps
   glancing over its shoulder) and 30% faster; the tortoise has twice the
   health. The raven **flies**: any journey longer than 30 m, any way that
   climbs steeply, and whenever you send it out of a fight. In the air it
   is two and a half times as fast, treads on nothing and cannot be reached from
   the ground. When it runs from a battle (see Actions in context below)
   it flies. Shorter trips on the flat it walks, and so does any trip with
   a load in its arms (wood, stone or food it is taking home). Each starts at 15% of its full size and grows for 30 minutes,
   getting faster, stronger and tougher as it does (a small one cannot pick
   gooblins up or flatten buildings, and stays out of raids unless you send
   it). Growing takes **animus**: it grows a tenth of the way at a time,
   and each tenth must be paid for first, 1 for the first tenth, 2 for
   the second, and so on (55 in all; `growth_animus_step` in
   `balancing.txt`). Left of its bars, a thin **growth bar** fills yellow
   as it grows, grey above what the animus paid so far lets it reach. Its
   first growth brings the **Bless** spell, and its second **Condemn**
   (they are not researched). It goes after any enemy within
   `creature_aggro_range` metres of it (45). Its **portrait** sparkles yellow
   when it has grown as far as it was paid for and the village has the
   animus for the next tenth: click it to pay. Hold the mouse over it to
   see how far it has grown and what the next tenth costs. It also stops
   growing while the village has no food. Double-click any of its bars to
   find it. Gooblins attack a
   creature that comes close; at no health it **dies**, shrinks by a
   quarter of its size (it grows back) and lies where it fell. A
   **Resurrect** button then appears above its bars: it costs 5 animus the
   first time and 5 more each time after, up to 25 (`resurrect_animus...`
   in `balancing.txt`), and brings it back to life beside your Shrine, in a shaft of light, with all
   its health. Left alone it heals.
   **Health, stamina and magicko.** Three bars to the right of the spell
   bar show the creature's own. The squirrel **runs** on all fours (long
   journeys, steep slopes, getting out of a fight): half as fast again,
   and no slope slows it. Running and the raven's flying use stamina, and
   stop when it runs out. The tortoise can do neither; it tires slowly as
   it walks, and walks at half speed with no stamina left. When it is hit
   it may pull into its **shell**, where blows do a fifth of the harm,
   until nothing has hit it for 5 seconds. Stamina, magicko and health
   each **level up** with use (stamina spent, magicko spent on its spells,
   health lost): the first level after 100, the next after 200 more, then
   300 more, and so on, but never more than 1,000 for one level. Each level
   gives 10% more of it, refilling 20% faster.
   **What it does by itself.** It wanders a little way from where you last
   sent it and comes back. Now and then it goes **exploring**: a long trip,
   up to half the map away, and back. The squirrel alone sometimes goes
   **forest seeding**: it tosses a magic acorn the size of a villager,
   sparkling with magic, somewhere within half the village's influence of
   its centre, and a **giant magic tree** grows there, as big as the
   Storehouse's tree in 2 days. It gives the village magicko: 10 a minute
   when tiny, rising to 100 a minute full grown. Now and then it drops a
   seed that becomes a sapling, and pixies (half a gnome's size, in a
   glowing green ball, trailing green dust) flit about its crown. Nobody
   can chop it down. After the first acorn the squirrel must wait 15
   minutes, after the second 30, and after that 60. It is a rare thing,
   so Bless and Condemn count 30 for it, not 10. In a fight the creatures
   and the balrog punch with one fist and then the other, and a gooblin or
   villager is thrown with one hand.
   **Saplings.** Now and then an unassigned worker or a lumberjack kneels
   for 20 seconds and plants a sapling near where they work, so the
   forest grows back; it grows into a tree in 2 days and is not chopped
   until then. How often is `saplings_per_minute` in `balancing.txt` (2)
   and the dev menu's Saplings planted slider. When gooblins raid, or hang about within 50 m of
   the village, it chases them, turning 30% faster in a fight. A line of
   text floats up from its head when it does something: the sentence for
   it from `Actions.csv` (the most specific one that fits, such as "The
   creature cast fireball at a gooblin. They probably deserved it."). In a fight it may **run from battle**: how hurt it is
   only matters as far as you have trained it to care (Bless and Condemn
   on the running-from-battle rows), and the rest is its nature: the
   squirrel, being nervous, runs half as readily again as the others.
   **What it learns.** Each time you cast Fireball, Rain, Roots, Chain
   Lightning or Blood Sacrifice the creature may notice (60% of the time
   for an average creature; the tortoise is more watchful) and gain 25
   knowledge of that spell, and now and then twice that (the tortoise and
   the raven are keener to learn); at 100 it has
   learned it and casts it by itself when it suits (fireball at a knot of
   enemies away from your buildings, roots on a crowd of raiders, rain on a
   fire in the village, a blood sacrifice when magicko has run dry and
   there are a dozen or more villagers, and rain on growing wheat as well).
   Each spell costs the creature its own magicko (25 to 35) and then rests
   for half a minute. Its fireball does your own villagers and buildings only a
   tenth of the harm. For every 100 lumber, stone or food your villagers
   carry into the store it gains 25 knowledge of harvesting that; once
   learned it pulls up a tree or a boulder (or reaps ripe wheat) and
   carries it to the Storehouse, 25 at a time when full grown. The
   tortoise is born knowing how to fetch all three.
   **Gooblin caves and buildings.** With no gooblin within its
   aggressiveness range, the creature goes for the nearest gooblin
   building or cave in it and hits it (a cave has 10,000 health). Once it
   is hitting a cave it keeps at it for 15 seconds whatever gooblins come
   (`creature_siege_seconds`), unless you order it somewhere; then the
   gooblins come first again. (Villagers turn on a gooblin at once.)
   **Bless and Condemn.** Press the **Bless** key or square (golden
   sparkles, 10 magicko): the pointer becomes a golden star and the top of
   the screen says what the creature last did (holding the mouse over the
   Bless or Condemn square shows it too). Click anywhere, within 30 seconds
   of that action, and its **affinity** for it goes up 10; press the key
   again to put the spell away. **Condemn** (red and creepy, 10 magicko)
   works the same way and takes 10 away. Every action has an affinity from
   -100 to 100, starting at 0. The creature cannot learn these two.
   **Waiting between judgements.** The same action can be blessed or
   condemned only once a minute; once its affinity is 30 or more (or -30 or
   less) the wait is 3 minutes, at 60 it is 6 minutes, and at 80 it is 12.
   **The action table.** `Actions.csv`, next to `project.godot` (your
   spreadsheet, saved as CSV), lists the creature's actions. Row 1 names
   each category (gathering wood, stone and food, stomping, throwing,
   eating, each spell, exploring, running from battle, hiding in its
   shell). Under it are its actions, the most specific at the top and the
   most general at the bottom. Each cell is the sentence the player sees,
   with what had to be true in square brackets at the end, for example
   `The creature gathered some wood. That was the lowest resource in
   stock. [Gathered wood when it was the lowest stocked resource]`. A cell
   with no brackets is the general action. A line break in a cell (a new
   line in the Google Sheet, Alt+Enter, or `\n` typed out) is a line
   break in what the game shows; in `Announcements.txt` write `\n`. In a sentence, `[enemy name]`
   becomes the enemy (gooblin, ogre, giant bat, balrog) and `[a or an]` the
   right word before it. The game reads each bracket by its key words and
   takes any numbers in it as written: highest, lowest, zero or at 0, over
   250 in storage, battle, combat or invaded; full health, over 80%, 50%
   to 80%, less than 50%, less than 25%; magicko under 25, magicko over
   300, housing available, no housing; enemy, enemy building, villager,
   tree, friendly building, fire, crops, wet, 5 or more, nothing or
   nobody. A bracket it cannot read is reported in Godot's Output panel
   and never applies. Bless and Condemn judge the first action down the
   column of what the creature last did whose condition held then and
   that is not still waiting; if every one is waiting, they say how long.
   **Mischief.** Some rows are things the creature should not do: fireball
   at a villager, a tree or your own building, roots or lightning on your
   people, a spell cast at nothing, a sacrifice when magicko is plentiful
   and there is room for more villagers (with magicko low, or no room
   left, a sacrifice is ordinary). It does them now and then, rarely at
   first; bless one and it gets likelier, condemn it and it all but stops.
   **Personality traits.** Each creature is born with a personality that
   lasts its whole life, and you train it on top of that. In
   `balancing.txt` each creature has five traits, written as percentages
   (100% is average): **flee** (how readily it runs from a fight),
   **mischief** (how readily it does mischief), **spellcaster** (how
   readily it casts any spell it knows; the raven is a natural, at 150%),
   **observant** (how often it notices you casting a spell or the
   villagers storing a resource, and learns from it; the tortoise is
   watchful, at 150%) and **knowledgeSeeker** (the chance it learns extra
   when it does; the tortoise and the raven are curious, at 150%). A
   knowledge seeker only learns new abilities faster: it changes nothing
   about its affinities or what it chooses to do. The notes in the file
   say what different values do. Two creatures with the same training
   still behave differently, and enough training can override a creature's
   nature.
   **How it chooses.** Every choice the creature makes is made at random,
   weighted by its affinities: the higher the affinity, the likelier, and a
   low one makes a thing unlikely but never impossible. What it does under
   a condition counts the affinities of the category and of each condition
   that holds, added up. A raven that runs from battle flies, and a
   squirrel runs on all fours (running and flying themselves are not
   judged). Only a long trip away from where you sent it counts as
   exploring: the higher its affinity for that, the more often it goes.
9. **Cast spells** with the number keys or the squares on the spell bar
   (each shows its spell's sign, the one the pointer turns into). Every
   spell must first be **researched at a Shrine** (the Singing rocks):
   click the Shrine to open its menu. The first costs 1 animus, the next
   2, and so on, and each takes 30 seconds (`spell_research_animus`,
   `spell_research_animus_step` and `spell_research_seconds` in
   `balancing.txt`), one at a time. The spell bar has room for 10 spells
   in a row, cast with 1 to 9 and 0 (or the numpad); the little **+** at
   its top left adds a second row above it, cast with **Ctrl** and the
   number, and a third above that, with **Shift**; the **-** beside it
   takes them away again. **Drag a spell** onto any place on the bar to
   move it there (onto another spell to swap them). Where you put each
   spell is remembered: next game, a spell you research goes back where
   you had it; otherwise it goes in the last spot, after the others. The spell goes into your
   hand and the pointer changes: a flame for Fireball, sparks for Chain
   Lightning, a cloud dripping water for Rain, roots for Roots, a skull
   dripping blood for Blood Sacrifice. Click to cast it (Shift+click casts
   and keeps it in hand); press the key again, right-click or Esc to put
   it away. Bless and Condemn are cast by pressing their key twice in a
   row, no click needed. Spells only work inside your village's influence, and a
   fireball that flies out of it fizzles out. After it is cast a spell
   **cools down** (`spell_cooldown_...` in `balancing.txt`) and cannot be
   cast again until it has. Meanwhile a see-through band rises over its
   square, reaching the top when it is ready, and its cost shows red. With
   Overcast it can be cast once during the cooldown for twice the magicko:
   the cost shows doubled, in blue with red lightning crackling round it,
   until it is overcast; then the lightning bursts outwards and goes, and
   the cost turns red. **Fireball** blows
   things up and starts fires, **Rain** puts fires out and soaks the ground
   so it can't burn for a while (and makes wheat under the cloud grow 4.125
   times as fast while it is wet, `rain_crop_growth`), and **Roots** grows a glowing green patch
   of roots for 20 seconds: gooblins and the tortoise in it are held where
   they stand, and your own people wade through at a quarter of their
   speed. The cost in magicko (the blue droplet) is under each spell.
   **Chain Lightning** strikes whoever is nearest the mouse and jumps
   on through up to twelve things, each within 9 m of the last: gooblins,
   ogres, bats and your own people alike, one jump every 0.15 seconds
   (`lightning_jump_seconds`; the creature's and the Storm Spire's
   too). Every wet thing it strikes (rained
   on lately, or standing in a river or lake) has a 75% chance of giving it
   one jump more (`lightning_wet_jump_chance`). The raven is born knowing
   it.
   **Blood Sacrifice** costs none: point at one of your villagers and
   they die, and magicko comes in twice as fast for 30 seconds (the counter
   turns red and drips while it lasts). A fireball crater is bare stone; it
   only fills with water when it breaks into water nearby.
   **Gnomish Subcontractors** (20 magicko; a gnome's hat for a pointer):
   click one of your buildings under construction and five gnomes, a
   third of a villager's height, pop up in sparkles about 50 m away, run
   to it very fast, bouncing as they go, and build it like five more
   villagers, moving round it every 1 to 3 seconds. They vanish when it
   is finished.
   **Kinky Fey Stuff** (100 magicko; four wings): click near a villager
   at work, and a pixie flies from the nearest tree to them; their eyes
   glow green and the two go off into the nearest building for 2 minutes.
   Then births come twice as fast for 2 minutes, and every child born then
   is a **fey** girl with four dragonfly wings, front and back pairs
   beating in turn. The fey fly (over water and cliffs too, landing only
   to gather), 50% faster,
   and make twice the magicko. Once there is a Lumber mill, **Fey
   Archers** can be trained too (up to the number of winged villagers, taking
   twice as long): they fly about, landing now and then but never with
   enemies inside the influence, shoot twice as fast, and drop green
   pixie dust (`fey_...` in `balancing.txt`).
   **Charm Offensive** (25 magicko; a pink heart): pink hearts float up
   all over the spot, and about half the gooblins caught in it (8 m
   across from the middle) are charmed for 60 seconds: their eyes turn
   pink and they fight their own kind, who fight back. Your people and
   your creature leave them alone while it lasts (`charm_...` in
   `balancing.txt`).

Days turn from dawn to daytime, evening and night.

## Neutral villages

Before a game starts (on the main menu, or while choosing your start
location) you pick how many **neutral villages** there are: at most one
fewer than the map has start locations. They take start locations you did
not pick, at random. Each starts as yours does, with a free Storehouse and
as many villagers, and then lives on its own: its people gather, eat, have
children, build and fight off gooblins for their own village.

- **What they build** comes from a build order file in the
  `NeutralVillages` folder: `Neutral_Build_Order_1.txt`, and any
  `Neutral_Build_Order_2.txt`, `_3.txt` and so on put there later (each
  village picks one at random when the game starts). The top of the file
  lists every building (asset name and gameplay name) with the most of it a
  village builds; below `[Build order]` come the buildings it builds first,
  one a line, in order. After the last it builds at random, up to the most
  of each. It skips one it is already at the most of, or has waited 5
  minutes for.
- **Their people:** 5 workers always (`neutral_keep_workers`; they build,
  and gather what the village has least of), as many warriors, archers
  and mages as their buildings can train (archers at their Lumber
  mill), and a third of everyone else on each of lumber, stone and food.
- **Worshipers.** Their people do not worship your creature at first. While
  your creature is in their village, one is won over every 5 minutes
  (`convert_minutes`); when it casts rain, or a spell at the gooblins there,
  or gathers near them, 1 or 2 more are won over at once
  (`convert_on_help_min` and `_max`). Worshipers make you magicko like
  your own villagers. At most 80% of a village can be won over peacefully
  (`worshipers_most`). Point at a neutral village's Storehouse to see how
  many worship the creature.
- **Taking over.** Once 75% of a neutral village worship your creature
  (`takeover_share`), it is yours: its buildings, people, army, stores and
  the ground round it. It is yours at once, too, when everyone left in it
  worships the creature, or when everyone in it is dead and its buildings
  still stand.
- **Gooblins.** Every neutral village there is at the start makes the
  gooblins stronger: 2 more gooblins a minute at every starting cave
  (`neutral_goblin_spawn`) and 40% faster building
  (`neutral_goblin_build_speed`). The gooblins raid them too (each raider
  picks your village or one of theirs). If they kill everyone in a neutral
  village and knock everything down, a gooblin cave opens where it stood.

## Map editor

Start it from the main menu with **New map in the editor** (it uses the map
size chosen above it) or **Edit map** on a saved map. A new map begins as a
random island with trees and rocks, but no villages or buildings: in the
game, villagers build everything.

| Tool (key) | Action |
|---|---|
| Raise, Lower, Flatten, Smooth (1–4) | Sculpt the terrain; Ctrl + mouse wheel changes the brush size |
| Expand island (5) | Hold and draw out over the sky to grow the island, in any shape |
| Sand (6) | Paint sand |
| Grass (7) | Paint grass; clears sand, stone and dirt, and fills in water |
| Water (8) | Flood the ground at the height where you clicked; the ground is flattened under it with a bank round it, so ponds can sit up on hills |
| Start location (9) | Click to place a numbered start banner (1 to 8 per map) |
| Gooblin cave (0) | Click to place a cave; R / Shift+R turn it first. Gooblins move in when the game starts |
| Trees | Hold to plant trees inside the brush |
| Rocks | Click or hold to place rocks |
| Erase | Hold to remove trees, rocks, start locations and caves inside the brush |
| Cut cliff | Click on low ground and drag into a hill: it is sliced down to that height, leaving a sheer cliff nobody can climb |
| Gooblin path | Draw from a gooblin cave to a start location (or back): raiders from that cave loosely follow it. Several paths can join the same two; each has its own colour, shown only while this brush is picked. Right-click (or Shift+click) removes one |

**Ctrl+Z** undoes the last change (each click or stroke is one change).
Pick the map's **colour palette** in the editor panel: the map is played
in it.

Water flows: lower the ground next to a river or lake and the water runs
in, and wherever water reaches the edge of the island it pours over as a
waterfall. When the land brush reaches the edge of the map square, the
editor asks whether to make the map bigger (64 m more on every side, up to
1024 m); nothing on the map moves.

Name the map and press **Save** (or Ctrl+S); a map needs at least one start
location. **Save and play** starts a game on it straight away. Maps are
saved as compressed files in Godot's user folder under `maps/` (on Windows
`%APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\SpriteSquirter\maps`); **Open maps folder** in
the main menu opens it, so maps can be copied and shared.

**Slopes.** Villagers, gooblins and the creature slow down a lot walking
uphill, and nobody can walk up or down the grey rock slopes
(`cliff_grade` in `balancing.txt`) except the squirrel; the raven and the
giant bats fly over them. Villagers and gooblins plan
their way to keep off steep ground and round cliffs and water, unless
there is no other way.

## Balancing

Numbers can be written as percentages too (`150%` is 1.5): the weights and
personality traits are.

`balancing.txt`, in the game's folder next to `project.godot`, holds the
numbers you may want to tune: the health and damage of villagers,
warriors, archers, wizards, gooblins, ogres and the creatures, building
health, what every building costs and how long building takes, bones,
necromancers and skeletons, how often the gooblins build, chain
lightning, the giant bat, the raven's flying, the balrog, the creature's stamina and magicko, the tortoise's shell, how steep a slope slows or stops walkers,
how many villagers build one building, training times, how fast farmers
work, how much stone a boulder holds, fireball damage, how much knowledge the creature needs to learn
each thing, the cost and cooldown of each spell it casts, the size of a bless
or condemn, each creature's personality (mischief and flee weights), and the soft
cooldown of your spells. Open it in any text editor, change a number, save, and start
the game again. Each line reads `name = number`, with a note after `#`.

## Announcements

`Announcements.txt`, next to `project.godot`, holds every line of text
the game announces: the messages in the middle of the screen (the raid,
the morning, the bat, research, the creature dying and coming back), what Bless
and Condemn show at the top, and the lines that float up from the
creature. (The sentence for each of the creature's actions is in
`Actions.csv`.) Each line reads `name = text`; rewrite the text after the `=`, save,
and start the game again. Words in `{braces}` are filled in by the game.

## What's in the world

- **Floating island:** a noise-based island with hills, lakes and a rocky
  underside, hanging in the sky with smaller islands drifting around it.
  Two rivers rise at a spring and pour over the edge as waterfalls.
- **Surreal buildings:** treehouse Homes joined by rope bridges, the
  crystal tower, the moon tower, the mushroom house, the singing rocks,
  the store-in-a-tree, the winged windmill and the skull-topped
  Necromancer tower. Your villagers build more from the build menu.
- **Gooblin buildings:** every 5 minutes (2.5 at the default gooblin build
  speed of 2), once the last one is finished,
  the gooblins start a building of their own near a cave: a tent, war
  hut, forge, potion hut or watchtower of planks, rags and bones. A few
  gooblins build it, going back to the cave for what they need, and they
  stay behind when the others raid until it is finished. Each finished
  one makes its cave spawn more gooblins; a fireball or the creature's
  feet knock them down.
- **Villagers and gooblins:** villagers wander their villages; gooblins
  loiter around a cave and their huts, far from the villages, until the
  nightfall raid. Both catch fire and get flung by explosions; the
  tortoise's footsteps squash gooblins and strangers but not your own people.
- **The creatures:** a 12 m gloomy tortoise of about 110,000 triangles,
  an 11 m nervous squirrel of about 65,000, a raven of about 155,000
  with two-jointed wings, on the same skeleton. Walking is procedural: feet
  plant on the terrain with two-bone IK, and the body, arms, head, jaw and
  tail react. The gooblins' balrog is a painted Meshy model (about 12,800
  triangles, one 2048 px texture) on a humanoid skeleton: it plays its
  walk clip as fast as it really walks, and code adds the rest (head
  turning, leaning, punches, stomps, its fall). Its lava cracks glow.
- **Fireball:** thrown from your cursor. It explodes, digs a crater, breaks
  buildings and trees into physics debris (Jolt), and sets fires that
  spread, char buildings and eventually collapse them.
- **Rain:** a storm cloud that puts out fires, burning buildings and burning
  villagers, and leaves the ground too wet to burn for a while.
- **Roots:** glowing green roots in a 9 m circle for 20 seconds: gooblins
  and the tortoise are held, your own people slowed.
- **Cel shading:** sunlight that is either on or off, with halftone dots
  where light meets shadow, dark blue-violet shadows, ink outlines, pen
  strokes hatched over the ground, a sun that never sits overhead, weather
  moods and swappable nature palettes (the default is "Alpine").
- **Detail up close:** villagers, warriors and gooblins switch to detailed
  models with inked faces, hands, belts and trims when the camera is
  within about 60 m, and back to simple ones when they are small.

(to look at, edit and animate) and `raven.glb` (what the game loads):

```bash
blender -b --factory-startup --python tools/raven_rig.py
```

The balrog's claw slashes are made in Blender
by a script, which writes `assets/creature/balrog_claw_slash.blend` (to


### Making a build other people can play

Godot exports a game to a file people run without Godot installed.

1. **Export templates (once).** In Godot: **Editor > Manage Export
   Templates > Download and Install** (about 1 GB, for this version of Godot).
2. **Presets.** **Project > Export... > Add...**, and add **Windows
   Desktop**, **macOS** and, if you like, **Linux**.
3. **The text files.** On each preset's **Resources** tab, put
   `*.txt, *.csv` in **Filters to export non-resource files/folders**.
   Without this, `balancing.txt`, `Names.txt`, `Announcements.txt` and
   `Actions.csv` are left out of the build and it runs on its built-in
   numbers.
4. **Windows.** Pick the Windows preset, tick **Embed PCK** on its
   Options tab (one .exe instead of an .exe and a .pck), then **Export
   Project...** and save `SpriteSquirter.exe`. Zip it and share the zip.
   Windows may warn that the program is unrecognised: players click **More
   info > Run anyway** (only a code-signing certificate removes that).
5. **Mac.** Pick the macOS preset, set a **Bundle Identifier** on its
   Options tab (for example `com.yourname.spritesquirter`), then **Export
   Project...** and save a `.zip` (or `.dmg`). It runs on Intel and Apple
   Silicon Macs. Unsigned, macOS blocks it at first: players **right-click
   the app > Open > Open** (or, on newer macOS, **System Settings > Privacy
   & Security > Open Anyway**). Signing and notarising with an Apple
   Developer account ($99 a year) removes that.
6. **Graphics.** The game uses Godot's Forward+ renderer: players need a
   graphics card from the last ten years or so (Vulkan on Windows, Metal
   on a Mac).

### Smoke test

A headless run through the menu, a random game (gathering, births,
building, rain), the editor (save, list, load), a saved-map game, a large
island, the raven and the balrog, neutral villages and the test
environment. With `--fixed-fps 60` every frame moves the game on by the same
step, as fast as the computer can go (about two and a quarter minutes;
without it, three):

```bash
godot --headless --fixed-fps 60 --path . res://tools/smoke_test.tscn
```

It prints `SMOKE TEST PASSED` or the checks that failed, and exits with 0 or 1.
It comes in eight sections (1 the menu, 2 a village on a random island, 3
the map editor, 4 playing its map, 5 a large island, 6 the raven and the
balrog, 7 neutral villages, 8 the test environment). To run only some, or
change the speed, add options after `--`:

```bash
godot --headless --fixed-fps 60 --path . res://tools/smoke_test.tscn -- --only=7,8
```

At the end it prints how long each section took and which waits took
longest.

### Conventions

- GDScript with static types everywhere (`var x := ...`, typed arguments).
- One `class_name` per script. Shared systems are reached through the `Game`
  autoload instead of node paths.
- Units are metres. Local +Z is "forward" for the creature and buildings.
- Tunable numbers are `const` or `@export` at the top of each script.

## Tech notes

- **Engine:** Godot 4.7, Forward+ renderer, Jolt physics.
- **Performance:** built for a mid-to-high-end desktop GPU. Grass is the
  heaviest part; lower it in the dev menu (~, then G). Debris pieces fade out after about
  10 s, and the number of fires and fire lights is capped.
- Trees, buildings and villagers are still individual nodes (about 300
  trees, 40 buildings, 100 villagers). Instancing them is on the roadmap
  for RTS-scale unit counts.
